# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Women 18–35, casually exploring wellness. They open this on a phone at night or a laptop on the weekend — not tracking KPIs, building a gentle habit. They're motivated by aesthetics and small wins, not clinical depth. The job: find one thing to do today that feels good and is actually good for them.

## Product Purpose

Quill is an all-in-one wellness companion covering body literacy, movement, skincare, nutrition, sleep, and daily habits. It exists to make evidence-based self-care feel beautiful and approachable — not medicalized, not gamified, not overwhelming. Success looks like a user returning every few days because the app feels like a treat, not a chore.

## Brand Personality

Warm, editorial, grounded. The feeling of a well-curated magazine you actually read, written by a knowledgeable friend who doesn't lecture. Confidence without authority. Calm without being sleepy.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS (Notion/Linear clones): cold neutrals, identical rounded-card grids, dashboard-everything, icon + heading + text repeated endlessly.
- Overtly feminine pink wellness (Goop, Ritual): pastel pinks, script fonts, crystal energy, over-precious and soft.
- Clinical health-tech (MyFitnessPal, Headspace): progress-bar dashboards, app-store blue, gamification badges, metric-first hierarchy.

## Design Principles

1. **Editorial clarity over dashboard density.** Every screen should feel like a spread, not a report. Hierarchy through type and space, not cards and borders.
2. **Warmth through material, not decoration.** The cream, clay, sage palette and serif display type carry the personality. Don't add ornament on top — let the system do the work.
3. **Less is more, consistently.** The product says it in copy; the interface should prove it. One primary action per screen. No feature stacking.
4. **Non-judgmental voice.** No streaks-or-shame. No progress bars implying failure. Design should feel like encouragement, not accountability.
5. **Seasonal rhythm over dashboard permanence.** The "Vol. 01 · Spring Edition" framing is the product's soul — design should feel alive, curatorial, time-aware.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Body-positive language and imagery throughout. No weight-tracking language. Respect reduced-motion preferences — all animations must honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
